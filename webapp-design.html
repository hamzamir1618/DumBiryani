Dum Biryani web app design

Version 1, 30 September 2026. Written for the team building the web app.

The phone app look called Poster was picked on 30 September 2026. This document carries that look to the web app and adds the logo. Everything here is live: the colours and type are the real values, and the three patterns in Immersion run inside this page.

Scope

One website with two parts.

  • Customer side, open to everyone. Menu, ordering for delivery and collection, table booking, order tracking, locations, account.
  • Staff side, behind a login. Live orders, kitchen screen, menu and prices, opening hours, sales reports. Same colours, plainer layout, built for speed and for screens that sit on a wall.

The phone app is a separate project and keeps the same colours and type. Where the two differ, this document is right for the web and the app document is right for the phone.

Logo

The logo is the word دم (dum) in Urdu above the word Biryani in English. Urdu is set in Noto Nastaliq Urdu, which is the flowing style used on signs and menus in Pakistan. English is set in Big Shoulders Display, the same face the app uses for dish names.

Rules

  • Keep clear space around the logo equal to the height of the letter B in Biryani.
  • The Urdu word sits above or to the right of the English. Never below it, and never inside a sentence.
  • Colours: saffron on dark, oxblood on white, white on oxblood. No other combinations.
  • Under 30px, drop the Urdu word and use Biryani alone.
  • Do not stretch, rotate, outline or add a shadow to any part of it.
  • Nastaliq has a long tail. Leave the space under the Urdu word empty; do not tuck text into it.
To check before launch. A native Urdu reader should confirm the word and the shape are right at every size. The tail of the م is part of the word, not decoration, so it cannot be trimmed.

Rules

  • No cream, beige or off-white anywhere. White is #FFFFFF and nothing near it.
  • Greys are neutral. They carry no brown or yellow cast.
  • Saffron is a fill, never text. Black sits on saffron, at 9.6:1.
  • Ember is a fill, never text, at 2.8:1 on smoke.
  • Customer pages run dark. Staff pages run white. Neither mixes the two grounds on one page, apart from a single band.

How much of each

  • Customer pages: about 55 per cent smoke, 25 per cent photographs, 15 per cent oxblood bands, 5 per cent saffron.
  • Staff pages: about 85 per cent white, 10 per cent black text and rules, 5 per cent saffron and oxblood for status.
  • One saffron thing per screen area. If two things are saffron, neither reads as the main action.

What does not flip

  • Photographs. A band with a photograph in it is dark in both themes, with white text and the same scrim. The pictures carry their own darkness and it would be strange to bleach them.
  • Oxblood. Selected pills, the page wipe and the ticker stay #6D1A1F with white on them. It is the one colour that anchors both themes.
  • Saffron fills. A saffron button with black text works on either ground and does not change.

The switch

  • Sun and moon in the top bar, next to the basket.
  • The new theme is clipped in as a circle growing out of the button, using the browser’s own view transition. Where that is not supported the theme simply changes.
  • The choice is remembered. On a first visit the site follows the operating system.
  • A small script sets the theme before the first paint, so a dark page never flashes white on load.
  • With reduced motion on, the circle is skipped and the change is immediate.

Urdu on the site

The site is in English. Urdu is used in the logo, and as an occasional accent next to an English heading. There is no Urdu version of the site, so the layout stays left to right.

  • An Urdu accent sits beside or above its English word. It never replaces it.
  • Nothing a customer needs in order to act, such as a price, a time or a button, is written only in Urdu.
  • Keep to a short approved list of words. Three to five across the whole site, not a sprinkle everywhere.
  • Urdu accents are saffron on dark and oxblood on white, in Noto Nastaliq Urdu at 28px or more.
  • Mark the text with lang="ur" and dir="rtl" so screen readers say it correctly.
  • Nastaliq needs about 1.5 times the line height of English at the same size, or the tails collide.
The three words above are a starting list. A native Urdu reader should confirm the spelling and the shapes before any of them ship, the same check the wordmark needs.

The rules behind the three

  • One photograph at a time, at full size. No grid of thumbnails. A photograph either fills the window or it is not on the page.
  • Type is sized to the window, not to a box. The opening headline is measured to the width of the screen, as it is in the phone app, so it reads as a poster on a laptop and on a phone.
  • Movement follows the scroll. Nothing animates on a timer except steam. If the visitor stops scrolling, the page stops moving.
  • Chrome gets out of the way. The top bar hides when the page scrolls down and comes back when it scrolls up. The basket pill stays.
  • Depth comes from layers, not shadows. Photograph, dark veil, then type. No drop shadows anywhere on the site.
  • Every dark band that sits over a photograph carries a veil of at least 55 per cent, so white text stays above 4.5:1 whatever the picture is.

What this costs

  • Photographs at full window size need to be sharp on a 27-inch screen and small enough for a phone on mobile data. Budget three sizes per photograph and modern formats, about 200KB each at the largest.
  • Scroll-driven movement is only transform and opacity, driven by one listener per page, so it stays smooth on a cheap laptop.
  • The first screen must still show its headline and its button before the photograph has loaded. A dark ground and the type come first; the picture arrives after.
  • With reduced motion turned on: the photograph stops holding, nothing drifts, the wipe becomes a 120ms fade. The layout does not change.

Staff area

Behind a login at /staff. White ground, black text, black rules. Oxblood, saffron, leaf and ember only as status fills. Rows are 56px tall so they can be read and tapped from a step away.

The staff side carries no decoration. Every pixel is a number, a status or a control, because staff read these screens for a whole shift.

Reduced motion

  • Every one of the above is either stilled or reduced to a short fade.
  • The held three-step sequence becomes three stacked steps that need no scrolling to read.
  • The custom pointer is switched off and the normal cursor comes back.
  • The page wipe becomes a 120ms fade.

Build notes

The front end in web/ follows this document. It runs on mock data, with no server behind it.

  • Next.js 16 with the app router, React 19, TypeScript, Tailwind CSS v4.
  • Motion (motion/react) for anything tied to scroll or to a shared element. Ambient loops are CSS.
  • Colours are declared once as the tokens in the Themes table. Components never name a raw hex.
  • Fonts come from Google Fonts through next/font: Big Shoulders for display, Instrument Sans for interface, Noto Nastaliq Urdu for the logo and the occasional Urdu word.
  • The logo is set in type, not an image file.
  • Photographs are free stock at 2600px on the long edge, served through next/image and blurred up from a placeholder built out of each picture’s own colours. They still need replacing with a real shoot before launch.
  • The basket survives a reload in the browser’s own storage. A placed order is kept for the session so the tracking page has something to show.
  • Built and checked at 1440px and at 390px, in both themes, with no horizontal scroll and no console errors on any page.
